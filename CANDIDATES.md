# Candidates — The Dog Reviews the Mailman

Brief (judge, verbatim): one funny one-line review a dog gives the mailman,
< 200 characters. Criteria in order: (1) on-subject; (2) the dog's side of
the visit — grievance, bribe, suspicion, small triumph; (3) a dog's voice,
dog's priorities and vocabulary; (4) one line, tight, turn at the end;
(5) the laugh comes from this dog and this mailman, not a pun that fits any
stranger at the door.

Pattern applied (skill humor-one-liner): twist in the final words; concrete
nouns + specific scene; never explain the joke; no field clichés.
Originality gate O1–O4 (skill makefun-short-text-originality): O1 exact-phrase
web search on the chosen line = 0 verbatim hits (26 Nov 2026); O2 — the
leaderboard is BLIND (scores after_finalize), so no rival texts could be
scanned — noted, not assumed clean; O3 structural — the chosen line is not
the cold-model first idea ("he never brings treats", "I bark at him",
"pee on the tire"); O4 — 6 candidates generated, first idea discarded.

## Chosen

**C1 (129 chars) — SUBMITTED**
> Five stars. He survived six years of my barking, one stolen shoe, and a formal complaint I left on the welcome mat. Professional.

Why: 3-beat escalation (six years of barking → one stolen shoe → formal
complaint), twist inside the final 8 words ("on the welcome mat" — the
complaint is implied, never explained), deadpan one-word kicker
("Professional."). Concrete nouns (shoe, welcome mat), dog's priorities
(territory, the mat), grievance + small triumph in one line. The joke is
specific to this dog and this mailman.

## Discarded

**C2 (139 chars)**
> Five stars. He comes every day, knocks twice, and still hasn't figured out I'm the one who signs for the packages by eating them.
Discarded: good twist ("by eating them", specific to signing for packages)
but only 2 beats and weaker escalation than C1; "knocks twice" is filler.
O1: 0 verbatim hits. Kept as runner-up.

**C3 (101 chars)**
> He knocked, I barked, he left a package addressed to the cat. One star, and I'm keeping the box.
Discarded: "keeping the box" is the first-idea dog trope (dogs love boxes)
— fails O3; the cat twist is nice but the punchline is the obvious one.

**C4 (91 chars)**
> He delivers bills, never bones, and still expects a smile. I left my review on his tire.
Discarded: "review on his tire" = the pee-on-the-tire joke — the single
most obvious dog-review gag; almost certainly convergent (fails O3).
Short and punchy, but the first idea every entrant writes.

**C5 (128 chars)**
> I gave him five stars for bravery: he walks up my driveway every day knowing full well I never signed the peace treaty.
Discarded: "peace treaty" is a decent twist, but the setup is abstract
("bravery", "knowing full well") with no concrete scene or object; the dog
is a narrator, not a participant. Weaker dog voice than C1.

**C6 (130 chars)**
> Two stars. He rings twice, runs away, and leaves paper that smells like other dogs. I ate the evidence and rated him anyway.
Discarded: "ate the evidence" is a generic punchline that would fit any
stranger at the door (fails judge criterion 5); "paper that smells like
other dogs" is the only mailman-specific beat and it sits mid-line.
